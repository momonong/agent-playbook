# Career-material editorial guidelines

Local synthesis, reviewed 2026-10-04. These references support writing principles; they do not establish hiring success rates or a universal layout. No specific wording, number of bullets, or role label is guaranteed to improve outcomes.

## Sources and their applicable principles

- [Harvard MCS: Creating a Strong Resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/): specific, active, factual, concise writing; easy scanning; consistent formatting; relevant accomplishments. Express the contribution clearly instead of decorating it with impressive language.
- [MIT CAPD: Enhance your resume](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2023/09/01/enhance-your-resume-a-guide-for-first-year-undergraduates/): project/action/result framing connects work with its outcome. Borrow the writing method; do not generalize the first-year audience's layout advice to other candidates.
- [LinkedIn: How to build a strong profile](https://socialimpact.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/socialimpact-linkedin-com/lifg-resources/linkedin-training-content/linkedin-profile-tip-sheet.pdf): concise experience descriptions centered on projects, accomplishments, and value; bullets can highlight accomplishments. About can use short paragraphs; do not apply one format to every section.
- [LinkedIn Help: Create a good profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351/how-do-i-create-a-good-linkedin-profile): include relevant work and education aligned with career goals, skills, and appropriate work samples. More space does not require including every project.

## Apply to resumes

Select evidence for the target role. A reusable base is useful, but a submitted version should reflect a real JD when available. Keywords must accurately describe experience. A number is useful when it explains outcome or meaningful scope, not merely because quantified bullets look impressive. Do not force every bullet to contain every part of PAR.

## Apply to LinkedIn

Choose a coherent professional narrative supported by the person's actual work. Experience entries can cover more context than a resume, but should still be edited for attention and relevance. Use a short scope sentence when it helps establish ownership. Then give distinct accomplishments, decisions, or implementation contributions without duplicating the same result.

Honor the user's chosen heading/bullet style. Neither one bullet under a heading nor a paragraph is inherently invalid. Evaluate density, repetition, hierarchy, and the actual preview. Unicode headings, heading counts, and grouping are editorial choices, not official recommendations. Do not claim to have inspected the live rendering unless actually inspected.

## Publication copy versus working evidence

Keep a source for every material claim in the working context. Put uncertainty into review notes when it is irrelevant to the reader; when it changes the meaning of a public claim, retain the necessary qualification or omit the claim. Scope evaluated results plainly without repeating long defensive clauses. Do not upgrade planned deployment to delivered deployment or existing system scale to new-feature adoption.

## Stop when ready for review

After the relevant facts, clarity, and requested format checks pass, provide a single reviewable draft. Do not keep introducing new frameworks or ask the user to reconfirm facts already supplied. A new factual correction or a concrete readability issue justifies another revision.
